# Anti-Gravity

> **Status:** Research overview. This page distinguishes established physics from hypotheses, historical claims, patents, and non-reproduced effects.

## Definition

**Anti-gravity** is the hypothetical production of a force or interaction that directly opposes gravity itself. It is distinct from ordinary methods of counteracting weight, such as aerodynamic lift, magnetic levitation, electrostatic suspension, buoyancy, orbital free fall, or ion-wind propulsion.

The central research question is therefore not whether an object can be made to rise, but whether the gravitational interaction can be reduced, screened, reversed, or made repulsive by a physical mechanism that survives controlled testing.

## Current scientific status

No reproducible experiment has demonstrated a controllable anti-gravity force. General relativity describes gravity through spacetime geometry rather than as an ordinary force with positive and negative charges analogous to electromagnetism. Standard matter and energy therefore do not provide a known engineering route to gravitational repulsion.

The strongest modern experimental evidence also disfavors a simple matter–antimatter repulsion scenario. In 2023 the ALPHA collaboration measured the motion of antihydrogen in Earth’s gravitational field and found behavior consistent with downward gravitational acceleration rather than repulsion [3]. Precision tests of the weak equivalence principle with ordinary matter, including the MICROSCOPE mission, have likewise found no violation at very high precision [17].

This does not prove that every conceivable modification of gravity is impossible. It does mean that a credible anti-gravity claim must overcome a substantial body of theoretical and experimental constraints.

## 1. Theoretical framework

### 1.1 General relativity and positive mass-energy

In general relativity, gravity is sourced by stress-energy and represented by spacetime curvature. Standard formulations do not contain a simple gravitational analogue of positive and negative electric charge. Textbook treatments of general relativity and quantum field theory therefore provide no ordinary mechanism for a body to carry a controllable “negative gravitational charge” [4][5][6].

A historically important analysis by Nieto and Goldman reviewed arguments against antigravity and the gravitational behavior of antimatter [7]. Earlier arguments by Morrison, Schiff, and Good examined conservation-law, equivalence-principle, and particle-physics consequences that would arise if matter and antimatter responded oppositely to gravity [14][15][16].

### 1.2 Negative mass

Hermann Bondi showed that negative mass can be treated consistently in certain general-relativistic solutions at the mathematical level [11]. The concept remains hypothetical: no material with negative inertial/gravitational mass has been observed as a stable engineering substance.

Negative-mass models are relevant because they illustrate that repulsive gravitational behavior can be written into some theoretical constructions. That is not equivalent to demonstrating that negative mass exists in nature.

### 1.3 Antimatter gravity

The historical idea that antimatter might “fall upward” arose because antimatter carries opposite electric charge for corresponding charged particles, while its gravitational behavior was difficult to measure directly.

The modern experimental picture is much narrower. The ALPHA collaboration’s 2023 antihydrogen experiment directly tested the gravitational motion of neutral antimatter and excluded the simple hypothesis that antihydrogen is gravitationally repelled by Earth [3]. The result is consistent with antimatter gravitating in the same direction as ordinary matter within the experiment’s uncertainty.

Alternative theoretical proposals have nevertheless been published, including CPT-based arguments for unusual antimatter gravity [13]. Such proposals should be treated as theoretical work unless and until independently confirmed experimentally.

## 2. Experimental search programs

### 2.1 NASA Breakthrough Propulsion Physics

NASA’s Breakthrough Propulsion Physics program examined unconventional propulsion concepts during the late 1990s and early 2000s. Mark Millis later summarized methods for evaluating claimed propulsion breakthroughs and separating viable physics questions from proposals that lacked supporting evidence [8][24]. NASA also published a screening document specifically addressing recurring “mechanical antigravity” claims [9].

The important methodological lesson is that a propulsion anomaly must be separated from conventional effects such as vibration, electrostatics, magnetic coupling, thermal drift, ion wind, instrumentation bias, and momentum exchange with the environment.

### 2.2 U.S. Air Force high-voltage testing

Robert L. Talley investigated electrostatic/high-voltage propulsion concepts for the U.S. Air Force in a vacuum environment [22]. The reported measurements did not establish a force above the experiment’s detection threshold under high-vacuum conditions.

This test is significant because many electrogravitic claims rely on thrust observed around high-voltage asymmetric capacitors. Vacuum testing removes or strongly suppresses ordinary atmospheric ion-wind mechanisms and is therefore a key discriminating test.

## 3. Electrogravitics and the Biefeld–Brown effect

### 3.1 Thomas Townsend Brown

Thomas Townsend Brown developed high-voltage capacitor devices that he interpreted as evidence of a coupling between electricity and gravity. His work became associated with the terms **Biefeld–Brown effect** and **electrogravitics**. The subject later attracted military, aerospace, independent-research, and popular interest [1][18][19].

The central experimental issue is whether the observed thrust persists when ordinary electrohydrodynamic effects are excluded.

### 3.2 Vacuum and replication results

Controlled investigations have repeatedly found that thrust from asymmetric high-voltage devices is explainable by ion wind or related electrohydrodynamic effects rather than by gravity modification. Tajmar’s 2004 analysis identified the Biefeld–Brown effect as a misinterpretation of corona-wind phenomena [20]. A much later experimental search by Tajmar, Kößling, and Neunzig found no gravity–electromagnetism coupling from steady electric or magnetic fields at the tested sensitivity [21][23].

Talley’s Air Force work also reported no force above the detection limit in high vacuum [22]. Taken together, these results place strong constraints on the claim that ordinary asymmetric capacitors generate vacuum thrust by modifying gravity.

## 4. Gyroscopic and rotating-mass claims

Gyroscopes produce counterintuitive forces and torques because angular momentum changes direction under applied moments. These effects can appear to produce anomalous lifting behavior when the full mechanical system is not considered.

Henry Wallace patented devices in the late 1960s and early 1970s that were claimed to generate a “kinemassic” or gravitomagnetic field using rapidly rotating material with particular nuclear-spin properties [28][29][30]. Patents document a claimed invention; they do not demonstrate that the claimed physical mechanism has been independently verified.

In 1989 Hayasaka and Takeuchi reported an anomalous weight reduction associated with one direction of gyroscope rotation [31]. A subsequent controlled experiment by Nitschke and Wilmath reported a null result [32]. Reviews of field-propulsion ideas have continued to discuss such claims, but reproducible anti-gravity behavior has not been established [33].

## 5. Superconductors, gravitomagnetism, and gravitoelectric coupling

### 5.1 Podkletnov gravitational-shielding claim

Eugene Podkletnov and R. Nieminen reported a possible reduction in apparent gravitational force above a rotating high-temperature superconductor [34]. The claim attracted substantial attention because it suggested a laboratory-scale “gravity shielding” effect.

Subsequent experimental work failed to reproduce a comparable shielding effect. Relevant tests include work by Li and collaborators [35], Woods and collaborators [36], Hathaway, Cleveland, and Bao [37], and later analyses of rotating-superconductor/gravitomagnetic models [38].

The current evidentiary status is therefore **unconfirmed and not reproducibly demonstrated**.

### 5.2 Ning Li and Douglas Torr

Ning Li and Douglas Torr published theoretical papers exploring couplings between superconductors, gravitomagnetic fields, and gravitoelectric effects [39][40][41]. These papers are part of the legitimate theoretical literature and are important historically because they provided specific mathematical proposals that could, in principle, be tested.

However, theoretical publication is not experimental confirmation. Later popular accounts described efforts to develop a gravity-related prototype, but publicly available evidence has not established a functioning anti-gravity device [42][43].

### 5.3 University of South Carolina “gravity generator” reports

Historical accounts describe a University of South Carolina technology-disclosure effort involving Douglas Torr and Timir Datta and a proposed directional “force beam” or gravity-generator concept [44][45]. The publicly available record is incomplete, and no reproducible public demonstration is known.

This material should therefore be cataloged as **historical program evidence**, not as proof of gravity control.

## 6. Gravity Research Foundation

Roger Babson founded the Gravity Research Foundation in 1948 with an early interest in methods of reducing or insulating gravity. The organization later evolved toward mainstream gravitational research and became known for its annual essay competition [25][26].

Its history is useful because it illustrates a broader transition in twentieth-century gravity research: early speculative interest in controlling gravity gradually gave way to precision theoretical and observational studies. Nobel laureates and major gravitational theorists have participated in the Foundation’s essay program [27].

## 7. Reproduction challenges and the Göde Award

The Institute for Gravity Research associated with the Göde Scientific Foundation offered a one-million-euro award for a reproducible anti-gravity experiment and reported unsuccessful attempts to reproduce various claimed effects [46].

For this repository, the relevant principle is reproducibility: a claimed effect should be considered credible only when it survives independent replication under conditions that eliminate conventional forces and measurement artifacts.

## 9. What would constitute credible evidence of anti-gravity?

A high-quality demonstration would need, at minimum:

- a clearly defined measurable quantity, such as force, acceleration, weight change, or field gradient;
- calibrated instrumentation with stated uncertainty and noise floor;
- vacuum testing when electrohydrodynamic or aerodynamic forces are plausible;
- electromagnetic, thermal, vibration, acoustic, buoyancy, and mechanical controls;
- reversal tests and blind or automated measurement protocols where possible;
- complete raw data and analysis sufficient for independent review;
- replication by an independent laboratory using a separately constructed apparatus;
- a force or acceleration that scales in a repeatable way with a controlled experimental parameter;
- conservation-of-momentum and energy accounting;
- consistency with existing equivalence-principle and gravitational constraints, or a quantitatively testable explanation for any violation.

A visually impressive lift or thrust effect is not, by itself, evidence of gravity modification.

## 10. Historical and cultural context

Anti-gravity has had a long parallel history in science fiction and speculative technology. The concept appears in early fiction involving materials or forces that weaken gravity, and later became associated with spaceflight, advanced propulsion, and “apergy” [47][48][49][50].

The cultural history is relevant because many experimental claims are communicated using language inherited from fiction or popular science. The wiki therefore keeps **cultural history** separate from **physical evidence**.

## 11. Research directions for this repository

TheFifthForce should treat anti-gravity as a structured research problem rather than a single claim. Priority investigation tracks are:

1. **Antimatter gravity** — precision follow-up to ALPHA and related CERN experiments.
2. **Equivalence-principle violations** — terrestrial, satellite, atomic, and quantum tests.
3. **Negative-energy / exotic stress-energy proposals** — theoretical consistency and energy-condition constraints.
4. **Gravity–electromagnetism coupling** — reproducible experimental limits and improved null tests.
5. **Rotating superconductors and gravitomagnetism** — complete reconstruction of historical experiments and modern replications.
6. **Electrogravitics** — high-vacuum, high-voltage experiments with force metrology and ion-current diagnostics.
7. **Reactionless-drive claims** — momentum accounting, thermal systematics, cable forces, RF interactions, and torsion-balance methodology.
8. **Historical program reconstruction** — patents, DTIC reports, NASA reports, university disclosures, and declassified records.
9. **Claim-to-evidence graph** — every major claim should link to primary evidence, replications, null results, and current status.
