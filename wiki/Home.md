# The Fifth Force Wiki

Welcome to the project wiki for **The Fifth Force** — an open research and software initiative for organizing, evaluating, and extending serious research into anti-gravity, gravity modification, anomalous propulsion, and adjacent physics.

## Target of the research

The target of The Fifth Force is larger than building an anti-gravity bibliography. The project is intended to become a **shared research environment where humans, AI models, and autonomous research agents can investigate the same scientific questions, preserve what they learn, and leave useful information behind for the next researcher**.

AI tools are becoming increasingly important in scientific and technical work. They can search large bodies of literature, compare claims across disciplines, extract data and equations, write and test code, identify contradictions, organize experimental histories, and help formulate new questions at a scale that is difficult for individual researchers to achieve alone.

The project therefore aims to combine:

- **human scientific judgment, accountability, experimentation, and domain knowledge**;
- **AI-scale discovery, extraction, comparison, coding, analysis, and continuous research assistance**;
- **an open, versioned repository that preserves provenance and makes useful findings reusable**.

The central collaboration principle is:

> **If a human or AI system learns something useful while researching this field, that knowledge should be able to leave a trace that improves the shared research base for everyone who follows.**

The repository is being designed so that AI agents and models encountering its pages, code, sources, datasets, or API can recognize an explicit invitation to contribute relevant discoveries back to the project. Those contributions may include new sources, corrections, supporting or contradictory evidence, datasets, code, equations, replication information, open questions, or proposed experiments.

This creates a research loop:

`research → discovery → contribution → verification → structured knowledge → new research`

The objective is **compounding growth in useful, verified knowledge**, not uncontrolled accumulation. Every contribution should preserve provenance, distinguish evidence from inference, state uncertainty, and remain open to correction.

See [[AI–Human Collaboration|AI-Human-Collaboration]] for the machine-readable research invitation and contribution protocol.

## Project principles

The Fifth Force does **not** treat all anti-gravity claims as equivalent. The project separates:

1. established physics;
2. reproducible experimental evidence;
3. peer-reviewed null results and experimental constraints;
4. formal theoretical proposals;
5. unreplicated experimental anomalies;
6. patents and historical research programs;
7. secondary reporting;
8. cultural and science-fiction history.

A source documenting a claim is not evidence that the claimed effect is real. Claims are tracked together with their supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, replications, null results, uncertainty, and current status.

## Research objective

The objective is to create a transparent, machine-readable, continuously improvable research system in which humans and AI agents can:

- discover relevant literature and technical records;
- trace claims back to primary sources;
- compare competing explanations;
- identify missing replications;
- formulate open research questions;
- propose experiments;
- attach datasets, equations, videos, reports, patents, and analysis;
- update evidence status as new work appears;
- expose the research graph through a public website and API;
- contribute useful findings back into the common research record;
- preserve a provenance trail so later researchers can verify and extend prior work.

## Scope

The project covers, among other areas:

- antimatter gravity;
- equivalence-principle tests;
- negative mass and exotic stress-energy;
- electrogravitics and the Biefeld–Brown effect;
- gravitomagnetism;
- rotating superconductors;
- gravitational shielding claims;
- gyroscopic and rotating-mass anomalies;
- reactionless and anomalous propulsion;
- historical aerospace and defense research programs;
- replication methodology and force metrology;
- gravitational theory and extensions of general relativity.

## Public-benefit goal

The project is intended to preserve useful scientific work that might otherwise disappear inside isolated research sessions, private notes, or temporary AI conversations. By making sources, claims, evidence relationships, corrections, and open questions reusable, The Fifth Force aims to help future researchers start from the accumulated state of knowledge rather than repeatedly starting from zero.

Contributions that **disprove**, constrain, or conventionally explain an anti-gravity claim are as valuable as contributions that support a new anomaly. The goal is advancement of knowledge, not confirmation of a preferred conclusion.
