# The Fifth Force Wiki

Welcome to the project wiki for **The Fifth Force** — an open research and software initiative for organizing, evaluating, and extending serious research into anti-gravity, gravity modification, anomalous propulsion, and adjacent physics.

This Wiki is the project’s working knowledge base. It is intended to show:

- the **project roadmap**;
- the **current implementation status**;
- the **scientific evidence base**;
- the **software architecture and documentation**;
- the **research methodology**;
- the **reference library**;
- open questions, replication needs, and future research priorities.

## Project principles

The Fifth Force does **not** treat all anti-gravity claims as equivalent. The project separates:

1. established physics;
2. reproducible experimental evidence;
3. peer-reviewed null results and experimental constraints;
4. formal theoretical proposals;
5. unreplicated experimental anomalies;
6. patents and historical research programs;
7. secondary reporting;
8. cultural and science-fiction history.

A source documenting a claim is not evidence that the claimed effect is real. Claims are tracked together with their supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, replications, null results, uncertainty, and current status.

## Research objective

The objective is to create a transparent, machine-readable, continuously improvable research system in which humans and AI agents can:

- discover relevant literature and technical records;
- trace claims back to primary sources;
- compare competing explanations;
- identify missing replications;
- formulate open research questions;
- propose experiments;
- attach datasets, equations, videos, reports, patents, and analysis;
- update evidence status as new work appears;
- expose the research graph through a public website and API.

## Scope

The project covers, among other areas:

- antimatter gravity;
- equivalence-principle tests;
- negative mass and exotic stress-energy;
- electrogravitics and the Biefeld–Brown effect;
- gravitomagnetism;
- rotating superconductors;
- gravitational shielding claims;
- gyroscopic and rotating-mass anomalies;
- reactionless and anomalous propulsion;
- historical aerospace and defense research programs;
- replication methodology and force metrology;
- gravitational theory and extensions of general relativity.
