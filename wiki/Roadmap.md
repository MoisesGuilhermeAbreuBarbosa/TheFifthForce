# Project Roadmap

> **11 September 2026 research update:** [[Research-Dossier]] develops five hypotheses with full mathematics, controls, source records and implementation gates. [[Research-Implementation]] distinguishes delivered capabilities from planned work.

This roadmap covers both the **research system** and the **software platform**.

## Phase 1 — Wiki and source foundation

**Status: in progress**

- Establish native GitHub Wiki structure.
- Create project Home, Roadmap, Current Status, Software Documentation, and Research Methodology pages.
- Publish the core Anti-Gravity overview.
- Seed the reference library from the source set used by the English Wikipedia article “Anti-gravity”.
- Classify sources by evidence type and evidentiary weight.
- Separate established physics, null results, theory, anomalies, patents, historical records, journalism, and cultural material.

## Phase 2 — Canonical research database

**Status: planned / partially developed outside this repository**

Create a canonical schema for:

- sources;
- claims;
- researchers;
- organizations;
- experiments;
- replications;
- null results;
- datasets;
- formulas;
- patents;
- videos;
- research areas;
- open questions;
- contributions.

Each source should have stable identifiers and structured metadata including DOI, URL, publication, authors, year, source type, claims, evidence status, and replication status.

## Phase 3 — Evidence graph

Build a machine-readable claim-to-evidence graph:

`Research area → claim → source → experiment → result → replication → current status`

Required capabilities:

- supporting and contradicting evidence;
- independent-replication links;
- confidence/evidence status;
- provenance for every statement;
- chronological history of a claim;
- explicit unresolved questions.

## Phase 4 — Literature Register

Restore and expand the Literature Register as a first-class research interface.

Required functions:

- search and filters;
- author/year/topic/source-type filtering;
- DOI and primary-source links;
- evidence classification;
- linked research areas;
- linked claims and experiments;
- dataset and attachment links;
- citation export;
- machine-readable records for AI agents.

## Phase 5 — Research-area pages

Create a dedicated page for each major investigation area with:

- scientific background;
- key claims;
- primary literature;
- positive experiments;
- null experiments;
- replication status;
- known conventional explanations;
- open questions;
- proposed tests;
- datasets, equations, and videos;
- related researchers and institutions.

Priority areas:

1. antimatter gravity;
2. equivalence-principle tests;
3. negative mass and exotic stress-energy;
4. Biefeld–Brown / electrogravitics;
5. gravity–electromagnetism coupling;
6. gravitomagnetism;
7. rotating superconductors;
8. gyroscopic anomalies;
9. gravitational shielding;
10. reactionless/anomalous propulsion;
11. historical aerospace and defense programs;
12. replication science and force metrology.

## Phase 6 — Open Questions and Contributions

The Open Questions system must support active participation rather than static display.

Required functions:

- submit a new question;
- contribute evidence to an existing question;
- propose explanations;
- add supporting or contradicting references;
- attach datasets, calculations, or experiment designs;
- discuss methodological weaknesses;
- track question status;
- preserve attribution and revision history.

## Phase 7 — Video research library

Build a dedicated video page with:

- embedded videos;
- title, author/channel, date, and URL;
- transcript or transcript link where legally/technically available;
- claims extracted from the video;
- research topics;
- referenced sources;
- evidence status;
- user submission of additional videos.

Videos are research inputs, not automatically evidence.

## Phase 8 — Research repository and downloads

Restore a rich research repository with:

- papers and reports;
- datasets and CSV files;
- equations and derivations;
- images and diagrams;
- patents;
- technical memoranda;
- archival records;
- downloadable Markdown/export functions;
- persistent source links.

## Phase 9 — AI / agent interface

Restore and expand the **For Agents** capability.

AI-facing documentation should provide:

- data schema;
- API endpoints;
- provenance rules;
- citation requirements;
- evidence classifications;
- contribution protocol;
- source-ingestion rules;
- claim-extraction rules;
- validation requirements;
- examples of valid structured outputs.

## Phase 10 — Public API

Expose stable read APIs for:

- sources;
- research areas;
- claims;
- experiments;
- people and organizations;
- open questions;
- videos;
- datasets;
- evidence relationships.

Future write APIs should use authenticated contribution and moderation workflows.

## Phase 11 — Research newsroom

Rebuild the newsroom as a continuously refreshed research-intelligence surface rather than a static list.

It should track:

- new papers;
- experiment announcements;
- replication results;
- relevant institutional releases;
- conference material;
- newly released archives and patents;
- important corrections or retractions.

Every news item should connect back into the permanent research database.

## Phase 12 — Reproducibility layer

For major experimental claims, maintain a reproducibility record containing:

- original apparatus;
- claimed effect size;
- measurement uncertainty;
- environmental controls;
- conventional-force analysis;
- independent replications;
- null results;
- raw data availability;
- current evidence status;
- proposed next experiment.

## Long-term objective

The final system should function as an **open anti-gravity research observatory**: a public, versioned, evidence-linked knowledge base where humans and AI systems can investigate unconventional gravity claims without losing the distinction between documented claims and demonstrated physics.
