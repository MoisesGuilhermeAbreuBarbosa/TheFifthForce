# Research Methodology

The Fifth Force is designed to investigate extraordinary gravity and propulsion claims without collapsing the distinction between **a documented claim** and **a demonstrated physical effect**.

## 1. Core rule

Every major statement should be traceable to evidence.

A research record should make clear:

- who made the claim;
- where it was published;
- what was actually measured;
- what uncertainty was reported;
- whether conventional explanations were excluded;
- whether the work was independently replicated;
- whether contradictory or null results exist;
- what the current evidence status is.

## 2. Evidence classes

### A — Reproduced / established
Results supported by independent reproducible evidence and consistent with the broader experimental record.

### B — Peer-reviewed null constraint
Controlled experiment that constrains, fails to reproduce, or rejects a claimed effect within stated sensitivity.

### C — Peer-reviewed theoretical proposal
Formal theoretical work that makes a physically meaningful proposal but does not, by itself, constitute experimental confirmation.

### D — Reported anomaly / unreplicated positive claim
A reported positive observation that has not been independently reproduced to comparable significance.

### E — Patent / historical program claim
A documented device, patent, technical program, or disclosure whose claimed physical mechanism is not independently established.

### F — Secondary / popular source
Journalism, books, interviews, websites, or historical commentary useful for context or discovering primary records.

### G — Cultural / fiction history
Material relevant to the history of anti-gravity as an idea, but not evidence for physical reality.

## 3. Source hierarchy

When multiple sources exist, prefer:

1. original peer-reviewed paper or official technical report;
2. independent replication or null result;
3. institutional record or recognized archive;
4. authoritative review;
5. patent or technical disclosure;
6. serious secondary reporting;
7. informal commentary.

Secondary material may be useful for locating primary evidence but should not substitute for it when primary records are available.

## 4. Claim normalization

Claims should be stored as explicit testable propositions whenever possible.

Weak form:

> “The device works.”

Better form:

> “An asymmetric high-voltage capacitor produces a net thrust in high vacuum that cannot be explained by ion wind, electrostatic interaction with the chamber, thermal effects, or measurement bias.”

The normalized form allows supporting and contradicting evidence to be attached to the same proposition.

## 5. Experimental review checklist

For every claimed force, acceleration, or apparent weight change, document:

- apparatus geometry;
- materials;
- operating conditions;
- voltage/current/field strength where relevant;
- temperature;
- pressure/vacuum level;
- rotation rate where relevant;
- mounting configuration;
- sensor type;
- calibration method;
- noise floor;
- uncertainty;
- data acquisition rate;
- raw data availability;
- controls;
- reversal tests;
- blinding/automation where practical;
- environmental monitoring;
- conventional-force analysis.

## 6. Conventional-force exclusion

Before classifying an observation as anomalous, evaluate at least:

- aerodynamic lift;
- ion wind / electrohydrodynamics;
- buoyancy;
- thermal expansion;
- convection;
- magnetic interaction;
- electrostatic attraction;
- cable forces;
- vibration;
- acoustic coupling;
- mechanical preload;
- bearing/friction effects;
- center-of-mass motion;
- electromagnetic interaction with nearby structures;
- sensor drift;
- data-processing artifacts.

## 7. Replication standard

A replication is strongest when:

- conducted by an independent group;
- built from independently sourced components;
- uses comparable or better sensitivity;
- follows a preregistered or clearly documented protocol;
- reproduces the claimed operating regime;
- includes controls capable of separating conventional effects;
- provides raw or sufficiently detailed data.

A failure to reproduce is only decisive within the parameter range and sensitivity actually tested.

## 8. Positive claims and null results

Positive and null results must be represented together.

The database should never display a positive historical anomaly without also linking known replication attempts and null constraints.

Likewise, a null result should not be generalized beyond its measured sensitivity or experimental conditions.

## 9. Patents

A patent establishes that an invention was claimed and legally documented. It does **not** establish that:

- the underlying physical theory is correct;
- the device produces the claimed effect;
- independent replication exists;
- the effect exceeds conventional explanations.

Patents are therefore historical and technical evidence, not experimental validation.

## 10. Theoretical proposals

Theoretical work should be evaluated on:

- mathematical consistency;
- compatibility with known experiments;
- explicit assumptions;
- conservation laws;
- limiting behavior;
- falsifiable predictions;
- parameter ranges;
- relationship to general relativity, quantum field theory, or other established frameworks.

A mathematically valid model may still describe physics that does not occur in nature.

## 11. Evidence status changes

Evidence classifications should be versioned.

A claim may move, for example:

`reported anomaly → replication attempted → null result → constrained`

or

`theoretical proposal → experiment proposed → positive result → independent replication → stronger status`

Every status change should cite the evidence that caused it.

## 12. Open questions

An open question should include:

- precise question;
- why it matters;
- known evidence;
- competing explanations;
- missing information;
- proposed discriminating experiment;
- required measurement sensitivity;
- relevant sources;
- current status.

## 13. AI-assisted research

AI can assist with discovery, extraction, classification, and comparison, but AI-generated statements are not primary evidence.

AI outputs used by the project should:

- cite source records;
- preserve uncertainty;
- distinguish quotation/paraphrase/inference;
- flag missing primary sources;
- identify conflicting evidence;
- avoid fabricating bibliographic metadata;
- expose the reasoning basis at the level of source relationships, not hidden chain-of-thought.

## 14. Corrections

Corrections should be explicit and auditable. Incorrect metadata, broken links, duplicate sources, mistaken interpretations, and evidence-status errors should be corrected without deleting the history of the change.

## 15. Research standard

The project is intentionally open to unconventional hypotheses, but the evidentiary threshold does not decrease because a hypothesis is unconventional.

Extraordinary claims are useful research targets precisely because they can be converted into precise tests, controlled measurements, and reproducible evidence.
